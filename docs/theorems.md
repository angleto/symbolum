## What this file is for, and what it is not

**A listing answers "is there already a lemma about this". A showcase answers "what can this system
actually do".** They are different questions and a reader arriving for the second one drowns in the
first: [lemmas.md](lemmas.md) holds every lemma the library publishes, and the pages here are
chosen so that somebody who has never opened this repository can tell from the statement why each is
worth having.

That criterion is written down rather than the selection, because a selection goes stale and a
criterion does not. It rules out the infrastructure a development needs on the way to its results,
which is most of the library and is not a lesser kind of lemma: `NUM_ADD_LT_MONOTONE` is load-bearing
and tells a newcomer nothing.

**Each page says what it does not have.** Only one development records its derivations today, so
most of the pages below have a statement, a foundation, a cost and no step-by-step account, and
each says so where the account would be. A page that quietly omitted it would read as a proof with
nothing inside it. `ABS_TRIANGLE` is the one with the full account, seventy numbered steps, and it
is in the showcase for that as much as for the inequality.

**What the pages are checked against is on the pages.** Each carries its own last section saying
which of its claims are derived from the theorem and which are not, including the three it
deliberately does not make: that the statement says what somebody meant, that a constant is spelled
the way the literature spells it, and that the theorem is worth proving.

## How to read a page

The statement appears twice on purpose. The first is set for reading and does **not** parse; the
second is the text the page was generated from, and it reads back through the same signature that
printed it, which `NotationRoundTripSpec` asserts over every statement in the library. That second
form is the one to compare against a textbook, and the one to paste into `Ask`.

Every page ends with the command that reproduces it. The claim is not quoted in that command:
`sbt` splits its arguments before the program sees them, so the shell's quotes are gone by then.
