# The reading register

**EXCERPT — for anyone scanning the index:**
Every axiom, every definition and every row of the precedence table that this repository's standard
chain holds, printed in the theory's own notation, each with whether a person has read it, who,
when, and against which source. Everything starts at **NOT READ**, because nothing has been. The
table below the marker is generated by `sbt "theories/runMain garden.xeno.symbolum.theories.Register"` and
pinned by `RegisterSpec`, so an axiom or a definition or a spelling added and not listed here fails
the build and the failure names that command. The attestations are hand-written in
[reading-register-attestations.txt](reading-register-attestations.txt).

*If you modify this file and this excerpt no longer accurately describes its content, you must
update it.*

## Why this file exists

[architecture.md](architecture.md) ranks where a change is dangerous, and the second entry is not
the kernel:

> **`Real.Axioms`.** A mis-stated axiom makes everything provable, and the only test that fails is
> the pinned copy in `RealSpec`, which fails because the text changed and not because it is wrong.
> Read a changed axiom against a textbook, out loud, and change the pinned copy in the same commit.
> The same goes for a definition body in `Real.Definitions` and for the notation: both decide what
> an axiom string means, and neither is checked by anything but a reader.

So for these three populations the entire defence is a person reading, and the reading had never
been done. Forty-five findings had reviewed the machinery of this repository and none had reviewed
its content: the trust boundary is enforced, the kernel is bounded, the proofs replay in a second
checker, and underneath all of it sit some statements that nobody has ever checked against a source.

A register does not check them either. What it does is make the reading a thing with a state.
Before this file, "has anybody read the axioms?" had no answer that could be looked up, and the
answer it would have had is the one this table now prints in every row.

## What a row is worth, and what it is not worth

A row that says a statement has been read is a record of somebody's claim. Nothing here verifies it,
nothing verifies that the source named says what the row says, and no test in this repository ever
will: a test that appeared to check an axiom would be worse than no test at all, because it would
retire the reading it was standing in for. The value of the register is entirely in the two things
it makes impossible, and it is worth being precise about which two:

- **A statement cannot enter the system unread.** A new axiom, a new definition or a new spelling
  arrives in the table as `NOT READ`, and its absence from the table fails the build.
- **The list cannot go quietly backwards.** A statement that is renamed loses its attestation and
  goes back to `NOT READ`, and the orphaned attestation is named by the failure rather than dropped.

Everything else, including whether any of these statements is correct, is exactly as unprotected as
it was. That is the honest description and it is the reason the register was asked for rather than a
test.

## The three populations, and which question each one answers

**The axioms** are read from `Chain.standard.widest.theory.axioms`, the whole chain and not one
development. That distinction is not pedantry: on 2026-09-11 a spec in this repository asserted a
lemma was missing by asking `Library.of(Chain.standard)`, stayed green, and the lemma had been
proved an hour before in a development that sat beside the chain. "Absent from this register" and
"absent from the system" are two claims. What makes the first worth something here is the scan
`RealSpec` runs over the sources: exactly three files may call `Kernel.assertAxiom`, so an axiom
outside the chain's signature would have to come from one of three named places. The shape of the
error a reader is looking for is a complete ordered field stated *almost* right.

**The definitions** are read from `Chain.standard.widest.theory.definitions`. A definition is
conservative and costs nothing in the trusted base, and that is not a reason to skip it: it decides
what the axioms *mean*. `x - y` is `x + -y` because a definition says so, `p \/ q` is a
second-order statement about what a disjunction is good for, and an axiom mentioning either states
whatever those bodies make it state.

**The notation** is read from `Chain.standard.widest.notation`. `NotationRoundTripSpec` checks that
`parse(show(t))` is `t`, which is the notation's consistency with itself and says nothing about
whether the grouping is the one a mathematician expects. An `==>` binding tighter than `/\` would
pass that test, and would silently read every conditional axiom in the repository as a different
statement.

## What the statements are printed from

Each statement is printed out of the theory through the chain's widest notation, not quoted from the
source text beside it, so that what a reader checks is what the kernel holds. The printer renames
bound variables as it goes, which is worth knowing before the reading starts: `COMPLETENESS` is
written over a predicate `P` in `Real.Axioms` and prints here quantified over `x:real -> bool`, and
`SELECT` prints the same way. The statement is the same statement; the letter is the printer's. A
reader who wants the source spelling as well as the term wants `Real.Axioms`, `Classical.Axioms` and
`Natural.Axioms`, and `RealSpec` and `ClassicalSpec` pin those against the theory.

## What is not a row, and why

**Constants that are declared and never defined.** They are listed at the end of the generated part,
below the three tables, and they have no `NOT READ` against them because there is nothing in a
declaration to read: an uninterpreted name proves nothing, and what each one means is said by an
axiom that is a row, or by a theorem that was proved. The list is there so that a constant with no
row is a constant somebody decided about rather than one that fell through.

**Type definitions.** `num`, `prod` and `word` are carved out by `Kernel.defineType`, which is
conservative and which a `Theory` records only as a type operator with an arity. The predicate each
one is carved out of is a defined constant and has a row (`NUM_REP`, `mk_pair`); the abstraction and
representation it introduces are in the declared list; the carving itself is neither, because there
is nothing in the theory value left to print. A reader checking those three wants the files.

**The structural symbols.** `(`, `)`, `.`, `,`, `:` and `->` are in `Notation.Structural` and none
of them has a precedence to get wrong.

**The kernel's ten rules.** They are the other half of the trusted base and they are protected by a
different mechanism: a bound on the lines of the four files, a second checker that replays every
proof, and the tests in `modules/kernel/src/test`. [architecture.md](architecture.md) ranks them
first for a reason; they are simply not what this register is about.

## How to record a reading

Read one statement against a source. Then add one line to
[reading-register-attestations.txt](reading-register-attestations.txt), copying the key out of the
first column of the table:

```
axiom ADD_SYM | Angelo Leto | 2026-09-12 | Rudin, Principles of Mathematical Analysis, 3rd ed., 1.12
```

and regenerate:

```
sbt "theories/runMain garden.xeno.symbolum.theories.Register"
```

Do not edit the table. It is overwritten by the command above, which is what the marker on it says.
