## The proof language, increments 3 to 6

`docs/adr/0070` planned six increments and `7.0.5` carried the first two. The other four:

- **Imports** (`docs/adr/0073`). A document opens with `import NAME` and sees exactly what it
  imports, directly or through another import. The documents under `modules/published` are a
  library of their own beside the chain's, regenerated into `docs/published.md`, and
  `Reproduced.sym` proves three of the five lemmas the ADR 0070 sample drew again, by induction,
  without citing them or anything resting on them.
- **The notebook** (`docs/adr/0074`). `theorem` is a command of a session: a cell holds one theorem
  with its proof, checked in the session's theory as it stands, and the session's named facts enter
  as citations, priced under `from this session`.
- **An editor** (`docs/adr/0076`). `modules/lsp` is a language server on its standard streams: the
  checker's problems as diagnostics, the goal each line was given as a hover, the declarations as an
  outline. The checker's `--json` carries the goals too.
- **Datatypes, recursion and `simp`** (`docs/adr/0080`). A document declares an inductive type,
  defines a function over it by structural recursion, and closes routine goals with `simp`.

## Peano arithmetic decides the closed bounded formulas

The object logic instantiates at any term substitutable for the variable, not only at a closed one
(`docs/adr/0078`), which is what let PA's axioms be used at open terms. On it, in `Deduction`: PA
decides every strict order between numerals, splits one below a successor, refutes one below zero,
and settles a quantifier bounded by a numeral from its instances, in all four directions. In
`Truth`: the truth of a formula with a numeral put for a variable, the class of bounded formulas
`NUM_BOUNDED` with the bound a variable or a numeral (`docs/adr/0040`), its closure under that
substitution, the rank `NUM_RANK` that substitution leaves alone, and on it `NUM_PROV_BOUNDED`:
for a closed formula of the class, PA proves it when it is true and refutes it when it is false.
`docs/where-it-stands.md` has the account; Sigma-1 completeness is what is not written.

## Equations decided by their ideal

`docs/adr/0071`: a system of polynomial equations is decided by membership of the goal's difference
in the ideal of the hypotheses, through a Groebner basis computed outside the kernel, and the kernel
checks the cofactors. Three records extend it. A denominator a hypothesis says is non-zero is
cleared, and a goal in the radical rather than the ideal is reached through `SQUARE_ZERO`
(`docs/adr/0072`). A sign `Linarith` proves joins the ideal as an inverse, so the order picks the
root the ideal leaves open (`docs/adr/0075`). And the ideal pins one unknown at a time, to a
rational root of its minimal polynomial, after proving from the equations that a denominator is
non-zero (`docs/adr/0079`).

## The rest

- **A refutation counts only the points where the premises held** (`docs/adr/0077`). `{x * x < 0}`
  with the conclusion `x = 7` came back as surviving a hundred samples with the claim tested at
  none; it now says the premises were false at every point that answered, `PremisesNeverHeld`, a
  case `Refutation.Outcome` gained for it. The sentences that counted the outcomes now name them,
  which `StaleCount` in CI holds to.
- **The model reads numerals at `num` and refuses an overflow.** `Model.Numbers` interprets
  `NUM_ONE`, `NUM_BIT0` and `NUM_BIT1`, so a claim writing a digit at `num` is no longer skipped at
  every point; a real in the model is finite by construction.
- **A refusal naming a proposition carries it as a term**, so it prints in the reader's notation.
- **Gilbreath's table**: the bound, Chase's Lemma 3.2 and the ladder, then the table over the
  primes, with the conjecture stated as the ladder at every window. `modules/draw` draws it beside
  Rule 30 and the XOR rule as SVG.
- **Decision**: a deterministic filter is a garbling, Le Cam's two-point problem has an exact value,
  and E5 with the kind of task revealed.
- **CI's coverage job reaches its verdict again**: the passes over the whole library are corpus
  measurements, which the gate skips, and a pass over a handful of lemmas keeps their lines covered.

## Upgrading

Changes that refuse or move something a caller of `7.0.5` could write:

- **`Check` and the notebook moved to `published`.** The commands are
  `tools/sbt "published/runMain garden.xeno.symbolum.published.Check FILE"` and
  `tools/sbt "published/runMain garden.xeno.symbolum.published.Notebook"`. The objects in `tactics`
  keep `Check.run` and the notebook's server and have no `main`.
- **`Refutation.Outcome` has a case more**, so an exhaustive match over it does not compile until
  it handles that case.
- **`NUM_LOGIC_SPECIALISE` and `NUM_LOGIC_REPLACE` ask `NUM_FREE_FOR i t p`** where they asked
  `NUM_CLOSED t`; `NUM_FREE_FOR_CLOSED` turns the old condition into the new one.
- **`Model.Quantity` has a private constructor**; `Quantity.of` answers `None` for an infinity or a
  NaN.

Nothing else here was written to change what a caller of `7.0.5` may write; that is the intent of
the changes listed, not a measurement over downstream code.
