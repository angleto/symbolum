# 0032. The certificate gains a square, and the products do not gain a degree

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-09-18

## Context

`README.md` says the proof side reaches *"Linear inequalities, and products of the hypotheses up to
degree three"*, and adds that degree four is the limit. Mycelium card `bacfaba5` exists to close
that line and asks, as its first step, which of three roads to take: widen the product schedule to
a parametric degree `k`; build a Positivstellensatz certificate with the multiplier search outside
the trusted boundary and the verification inside, which is `docs/adr/0003`'s arrangement; or decide
real closed fields outright with CAD, which is the only one of the three that removes the word
"limit" from the line.

`docs/adr/0029` is what makes the question urgent rather than tidy. It enumerates the eighteen
miniF2F statements this system attempts and cannot settle, and it names two of them as waiting on
this card alone, being the cheapest thing on its list and the only goals whose refusal already
names the obstacle.

## The measurement that decides it, and it is not about degree

Read off the sources at `6a4a3ef` and off the corpus this repository vendors.

**The two goals have no hypotheses at all.** `third-party/minif2f/lean/src/test.lean`:

| name | statement |
|---|---|
| `algebra_sqineq_at2malt1` | `a * (2 - a) <= 1` |
| `mathd_algebra_113` | `x^2 - 14 * x + 3 >= 7^2 - 14 * 7 + 3` |

**And `Linarith`'s fact pool is generated by the goal.** `proveBody` builds it with
`traverse(shape.hypotheses)(LinearFact.of(reals, _))` and nothing else; every other route adds a
fact read off the goal in the same way, `fromCase` a disjunct of a trichotomy split and `byDenial`
the denied conclusion. No route seeds a fact that is not in the text it was handed.

Those two sentences together are the whole argument. **A goal with no hypotheses has an empty pool,
an empty pool has no products at any degree, and so widening the schedule from three to `k` settles
neither of the two goals for any `k`.** That is not a judgement about cost. It is arithmetic about
an empty set, and it disposes of the first road for the population that asked the question.

**What both goals need is one square, and it is the same square twice.** `a * (2 - a) <= 1` is
`0 <= a * a - 2 * a + 1`, which is `(a - 1) * (a - 1)`; `x^2 - 14 * x + 3 >= 7^2 - 14 * 7 + 3` is
`0 <= x * x - 14 * x + 49`, which is `(x - 7) * (x - 7)`. `Order.SQUARE_NONNEG` is that lemma and
this library holds it.

**And the square is all that is needed, because the rest is already linear.** `LinearForm` reads a
product of unknowns as an atom, so over the atoms `a` and `a * a` the goal is the linear
`2 * a - a * a <= 1` and the square is the linear `a * a - 2 * a + 1 >= 0`. They are the same
statement. The certificate is one fact at multiplier one, and the machinery that finds it is the
one already there.

**`SQUARE_NONNEG` appears nowhere in `Linarith.scala`**, which is the absence this record is about.
The control on that reading is in the same file with the same tool: `multiplyNonNegative` is found
at lines 861 and 862, so the instrument reads this file and the zero is an absence rather than a
silence.

## Decision

**The fact pool gains a source, and the first source is a completed square. The product schedule
keeps its degree of three.**

1. **What is extended is the pool, not the certificate.** `Certificate.find` takes a vector of
   `LinearFact` and returns multipliers indexed against it, and `productsOf` already runs over a
   pool that the cubic round has extended with manufactured facts. So a fact that is not a
   hypothesis is a shape this procedure already has. A **source** is a function producing a
   `LinearFact` together with the `Kernel.Thm` that justifies it, which is the pair the pool and
   its theorem vector already hold side by side. Writing it that way costs nothing today and is
   what makes the next increment additive rather than a rewrite.
2. **The first source completes the square**, justified by `Order.SQUARE_NONNEG` and therefore
   checked by the kernel like every other fact: over the atoms the goal already names,
   `x * x + b * x + c` is `(x + b/2) * (x + b/2) + (c - b * b / 4)`, and the coefficients are the
   rationals `LinearForm` carries. Exact, no search outside the boundary, no numeric library, no
   floating point, because the quadratic case is linear algebra rather than an optimisation.
3. **The second source is a semidefinite search and is not built now.** A general sum-of-squares
   certificate wants one, which belongs outside the trusted boundary with its answer checked
   inside, exactly as `docs/adr/0003` prescribes and `Propositional` already does. It is a second
   increment with its own record, and it lands behind the same interface as the first: of the
   eighteen, `docs/adr/0029` puts six under "a genuinely hard inequality", and those are where it
   would be spent.
4. **`support` keeps answering about hypotheses, and this change puts it at risk.** It is the
   diagnostic half of this procedure, the one an article and `Domain` want, and it answers which of
   a goal's hypotheses the proof rests on by rebuilding a certificate over `shape.hypotheses`
   alone. A proof that spent a square is one that certificate cannot reproduce, so `support` must
   carry the square when it rebuilds and report it **beside** the hypotheses rather than among
   them. Left alone it refuses a goal that was just proved, with the message that says exactly
   that, which is loud and wrong; made to report the square as a premise it would be quiet and
   worse. Either repair is a decision, and this is it.
5. **The products stay at three, and not because the corpus stops there.** Three of the eighteen
   carry a monomial above degree three, counted by reading all eighteen rather than by a pattern:
   `imo_1983_p6` at four (`a^2 * b * (a - b)` is `a^3 * b - a^2 * b^2`), `mathd_algebra_362` at five
   and `aime_1990_p15` at six. What the schedule stays at three for is that a fourth enumeration
   round is the wrong instrument for all three: two of them are systems to be solved rather than
   inequalities to be certified, and the third is in the class point 3 sends to a certificate
   search, where the degree of the products is a parameter of that search rather than another round
   of this one. `Linarith.byCubes` records separately that the cubic round is affordable only
   because it is last.

## Consequences

**The refusal has to say which of the two obstacles it met, and today it says the wrong one.**
`Linarith.naming` fires when a goal's atom is outside the products' reach and has more than one
factor, and it says *"the hypotheses multiplied reach three"*. On a goal with no hypotheses that
sentence is true and misleading in the way this repository cares about: the reader is told about a
degree and sent to restate the goal, when the pool is empty and no restatement of that kind exists.
A goal whose pool is empty gets a message naming the pool. This is `LinearForm.unreadable`'s
discipline one limit further along, which is the argument `naming`'s own docstring already makes.

**The README line changes and does not become shorter.** It stops saying degree four is the limit
and names the fragment: linear inequalities over the atoms, products of the hypotheses to degree
three, and a square completed over a quadratic atom. What is outside it is still refused by name,
which is the half of the card that must survive whatever else happens.

**Two of the eighteen move and sixteen do not**, and the record says so in front rather than
leaving a reader to discover it. `docs/adr/0029`'s table is unchanged for the other three rows.

**Rejected: widening the product schedule to a parametric degree `k`.** It is the short road and
the card lists it first. It settles neither goal that asked for this work, for the reason measured
above, and it is quartic in the pool where the cubic round is already the expensive one. A road
that costs more and delivers nothing on the population that motivated it is not a cheaper version
of the decision taken here. What it is **not** rejected for is that the corpus has nothing above
degree three, which is false and is counted in the decision above.

**Rejected: CAD, the decision procedure for real closed fields.** It is the only one of the three
that would remove the word "limit", and it is refused for a reason that is not its size. CAD does
not emit a certificate: its answer is the outcome of a computation over cell decompositions, and
nothing falls out of it that the kernel can check. Adopting it would mean either trusting it, which
is what this repository is arranged to avoid, or reconstructing proofs from it, which is a research
problem rather than an implementation. `docs/adr/0010` already refuses a computation rule in the
kernel for the neighbouring reason.

**What this does not say.** It does not claim the two goals are proved: it decides what to build,
and the building is the card's step 3. It does not rank the three things `docs/adr/0029` says the
corpus asks for. And the completion of the square is decided for a **quadratic** atom, so a goal
needing a square of a form that is not linear in the atoms is outside it and is refused by name.

**What would reopen this.** A goal measured to be settled by a fourth degree of products and by
nothing cheaper, which is a measurement nobody has made in either direction and which this record
does not claim to have made; or a body of goals in the "genuinely hard inequality" class becoming
the work in hand, which is what would buy the semidefinite search and the numeric library that
goes with it. There is deliberately no card for the fourth degree on its own: when it is wanted it
will be wanted as a parameter of that search, and a card for a fourth enumeration round would be a
card for the instrument this record declines.
